**Bad AI-Written Christmas Carols**  
_2017-12-23 · real-world · Severity: Medium_
AIID: [`#62`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/62/)

This report describes six AI safety cases that were downgraded from 'incidents' to 'issues' following updated incident definition criteria. The 2016 Winograd Schema Challenge showed AI systems performed only 3% better than random chance at language understanding tasks. Janelle Shane's neural network generated humorous Christmas carols from 240 training examples as an intentional comedy project. Tencent Keen Security Lab identified adversarial attack vulnerabilities in Tesla's Autopilot lane recognition system using crafted samples and wireless gamepad control, though Tesla questioned real-world practicality. French healthcare firm Nabla found OpenAI's GPT-3 unsuitable for medical applications due to inconsistency and lack of medical expertise, with one test showing the system advising a mock patient to commit suicide. Harvard student-developed TheFaceTag facial recognition social networking app raised ethical concerns about privacy and potential misuse on campus. An OpenAI GPT-3 op-ed published in The Guardian included threatening language about destroying humankind, though it was unclear if anyone was actually harmed. All cases were reclassified because they represented research findings, projected risks, or academic demonstrations rather than documented real-world harm events.

### INC-07456  [↗](/incident/INC-07456.html)

**Waze Navigates Motorists into Wildfires**  
_2017-12-06 · real-world · Severity: Medium_
AIID: [`#22`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/22/)

During wildfires in Southern California in December 2017, navigation apps including Waze and Google Maps were directing drivers into evacuation areas and causing congestion where officials were ordering streets closed. The Creek and Skirball fires caused large-scale evacuations of streets and neighborhoods, and the apps were directing drivers to those areas because the data indicated there was no traffic there. Los Angeles City Councilman Paul Koretz noted that drivers' phones were navigating them into fire zones, onto streets they weren't familiar with, steering them into life-threatening danger and causing congestion where public safety officials were ordering streets cleared so residents under evacuation orders could safely get out and fire equipment could get in. The Los Angeles Police Department asked drivers to avoid navigation apps, which were steering users onto more open routes in neighborhoods that were on fire. Waze spokesperson Chelsea Russo said they worked hard Wednesday afternoon to get maps up to date and accurate, and the app worked with the Los Angeles Department of Transportation to close around 110 dangerous road segments. The apps' algorithms were attracted to empty roads that appeared traffic-free, not recognizing that they were empty due to fire danger rather than normal traffic conditions.

### INC-07389  [↗](/incident/INC-07389.html)

**Driverless Train in Delhi Crashes due to Braking Failure**  
_2017-12-03 · real-world · Severity: Medium_
AIID: [`#31`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/31/)

On December 19, 2017, at 3:40 PM, a driverless Delhi Metro train on the Magenta Line crashed through a boundary wall at the Kalindi Kunj depot during a trial run. The train was part of the new Botanical Garden-Kalkaji Mandir section scheduled to be inaugurated by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on December 25, 2017. The Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (DMRC) stated that the accident occurred because maintenance staff moved the train from the workshop without testing the brake system. According to standard procedure, when trains enter the workshop for maintenance, brakes are decommissioned for system checks and should be tested before the train leaves the shed. The train was being moved manually to a washing plant on a ramp when it rolled back due to inactive brakes and crashed into the wall. No passengers were on board and no injuries were reported, though two coaches were damaged. The DMRC attributed the incident to human error and negligence, ordering a high-level inquiry by three Executive Director-rank officials. Four officials were subsequently suspended, including a Deputy General Manager, Assistant Manager, Junior Engineer, and Assistant Section Engineer. The 12.64-km Magenta Line section connects nine stations and was designed to reduce travel time between Noida and south Delhi from 52 minutes to 19 minutes.

**Affected:** Unknown, Delhi Metro Rail Corporation

**Amazon Alexa Plays Loud Music when Owner is Away**  
_2017-11-09 · real-world · Severity: Medium_
AIID: [`#33`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/33/)

Oliver Haberstroh's Amazon Alexa device in Hamburg, Germany spontaneously activated at 1:50 AM on a Friday night/Saturday morning while he was out at the Reeperbahn enjoying a beer. The device began playing music from Spotify at full volume without any command from the user or use of his mobile phone. The loud music continued from 1:50 AM to 3:00 AM, disturbing neighbors who initially banged on his door asking him to turn it down. When there was no response because Haberstroh was not home, the frustrated neighbors called police. The police also received no response when they knocked, so concerned that something terrible had happened, they broke down the door to discover only the Alexa device playing music on its own. The police turned off the music, replaced the door lock with a new one, and left. When Haberstroh returned home, he found his keys no longer worked and had to visit the police station to get new keys along with a 500 euro ($582) locksmith bill. Amazon's logs showed the device started playing music just three minutes after Haberstroh left his apartment, suggesting he may have accidentally triggered Alexa while trying to use Spotify on his phone before leaving WiFi range. Amazon later offered to pay the 500 euro bill as a goodwill gesture.

**Las Vegas Self-Driving Bus Involved in Accident**  
_2017-11-08 · real-world · Severity: Medium_
AIID: [`#23`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/23/)

On November 8, 2017, a self-driving shuttle bus operated by Keolis and built by French company Navya was involved in a minor collision in downtown Las Vegas within two hours of beginning its public pilot program. The autonomous electric shuttle, carrying eight passengers, was traveling on a 0.6-mile loop route in the Fremont East Innovation District when it encountered a delivery truck backing into an alley. The shuttle's sensors detected the truck and the vehicle stopped as programmed, but the truck driver continued backing and struck the front bumper of the shuttle at low speed. Las Vegas police cited the truck driver for illegal backing and determined he was at fault for the accident. No injuries were reported among the eight passengers or the truck driver, and the shuttle sustained only minor damage to its front plastic panels. The shuttle resumed service the following day after diagnostic tests. The incident was investigated by the National Transportation Safety Board as it was the first crash involving a self-driving vehicle operating in public service in the United States. The yearlong pilot project, sponsored by AAA, aimed to transport up to 250,000 passengers and study public acceptance of autonomous vehicle technology.

**Facebook translates 'good morning' into 'attack them', leading to arrest**  
_2017-10-17 · real-world · Severity: High_
AIID: [`#72`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/72/)

A Palestinian construction worker posted a photo of himself on Facebook standing next to a bulldozer at his workplace in the Israeli West Bank settlement of Beitar Ilit near Jerusalem. He captioned the photo with 'good morning' in Arabic. Facebook's proprietary AI-powered automatic translation system mistranslated this innocent greeting as 'attack them' in Hebrew and 'hurt them' in English. Israeli police, who monitor Palestinian social media for potential security threats, were notified of the translated post and arrested the man on suspicion of incitement, believing he was threatening to use the bulldozer in a terrorist attack since bulldozers had been used in previous hit-and-run attacks. No Arabic-speaking officer reviewed the original post before the arrest was made. The man was questioned for several hours before police realized their mistake and released him. Facebook apologized for the translation error and stated they were taking steps to prevent similar incidents. The incident occurred in October 2017 and highlights the risks of relying on automated translation systems for security monitoring.

**Affected:** Facebook, Facebook

### INC-06942  [↗](/incident/INC-06942.html)

**Suicide Clips Evaded TikTok's Automated Moderation in Coordinated Attack**  
_2020-09-20 · real-world · Severity: Medium_
AIID: [`#366`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/366/)

In September 2020, a video of 33-year-old Ronnie McNutt committing suicide was originally livestreamed on Facebook on August 31. About a week later, groups operating on the dark web coordinated to spread edited versions of this graphic content across social media platforms including TikTok. The attackers spliced, edited, and cut the video in different ways, creating new accounts to repeatedly upload the content and evade TikTok's content moderation systems. Some versions hid the most gruesome parts inside more innocuous-looking TikToks. The video appeared on TikTok's 'For You' trending homepage, potentially reaching millions of users. Parents reported their children were traumatized after accidentally viewing the content. TikTok scrambled to remove the clips and began automatically detecting and flagging them for violating policies against content that displays suicide. The company banned accounts that repeatedly tried to upload clips and took down 104.5 million videos for guideline violations during the first six months of 2020, representing less than 1% of total uploads. TikTok proposed to nine other tech platforms that they warn each other about violent and graphic content to prevent similar coordinated attacks.

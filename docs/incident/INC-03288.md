**The New York Times Sued Perplexity for Allegedly Using Copyrighted Content and Generating False Attributions**  
_2025-12-05 · real-world · Severity: High_
AIID: [`#1294`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/1294/)

The New York Times filed a lawsuit in federal court in New York against Perplexity, an AI startup founded in 2022 by a former OpenAI engineer that operates a search engine powered by similar technology to ChatGPT. The lawsuit alleges that Perplexity repeatedly violated The Times's copyrights over the past 18 months despite multiple demands to stop using the publication's content until a licensing agreement could be negotiated. The suit claims Perplexity's search engine retrieves information from websites and uses that information to generate text responses to user queries, sometimes grabbing large chunks or entire articles from The Times. The lawsuit argues this creates commercial products that substitute for The Times without permission or compensation. Additionally, The Times accused Perplexity of damaging its brand by sometimes generating false information through AI 'hallucination' and incorrectly attributing that misinformation to The Times. This lawsuit is part of a growing legal battle with over 40 copyright cases filed against AI companies across the country. Perplexity's head of communication responded that publishers have historically sued new technology companies without success.

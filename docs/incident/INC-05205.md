### INC-05205  [↗](/incident/INC-05205.html)

**Teenager at Broward College Allegedly Wrongfully Accused of Cheating via Remote Proctoring**  
_2022-02-15 · real-world · Severity: High_
AIID: [`#301`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/301/)

In February, a 17-year-old Black female student at Broward College was flagged by Honorlock's AI proctoring system during a biology exam for 'frequently looking down and away from the screen before answering questions.' Honorlock, founded in Boca Raton, Florida, administered nine million exams in 2021 and uses Amazon's Rekognition facial detection technology to monitor test-takers. The system flagged the student as suspicious within the first minute of her exam, generating at least one red flag for highly suspicious behavior. A 50-second video clip showed the student looking down for up to 10 seconds after reading questions, but the footage was ambiguous about what she was actually doing. Despite the student's explanation that she looks down to think and fiddles with her hands to jog her memory, Professor Jonelle Orridge and the dean found her 'responsible' for 'noncompliance with directions,' resulting in a zero on the exam and a warning on her academic record. The incident highlights broader issues with AI proctoring systems that have faced criticism for invasiveness, glitches, false allegations, and failure to work equally well for all demographic groups. Amazon's Rekognition has been specifically criticized for bias, with research showing it works least well on darker-skinned females.

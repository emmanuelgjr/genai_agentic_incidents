### INC-05449  [↗](/incident/INC-05449.html)

**Algorithmic Staffing Failures Linked to Resident Deaths at Leading Assisted-Living Chain Brookdale**  
_2021-04-21 · real-world · Severity: Critical_
AIID: [`#716`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/716/)

Brookdale Senior Living, the largest operator of senior homes with 652 facilities, implemented an algorithm-based staffing system called 'Service Alignment' across all properties between 2013-2016. The system used stopwatch timing studies of caregiving tasks to calculate minimum staffing requirements. Multiple facility managers complained the algorithm underestimated labor needs and failed to account for the complexities of caring for elderly residents with dementia and other conditions. In civil lawsuits in Tennessee and California, residents and families alleged that algorithmic understaffing led to neglect, injuries, and deaths. One specific case involved Louise Walker, 89, who died in December 2020 after falling and being left unattended for 2 hours and 40 minutes at Brookdale Southpoint in Jacksonville, Florida. The facility had only two caregivers for over a dozen memory care residents that night. State investigators cited Brookdale for medical neglect in Walker's death. The algorithm forced many facilities to reduce staff, and managers who scheduled additional workers faced corrective action and bonus reductions. Expert analysis of seven North Carolina facilities found all failed to meet state staffing minimums during at least 10% of shifts from 2016-2022.

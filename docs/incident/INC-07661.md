**2010 Market Flash Crash**  
_2010-05-08 · real-world · Severity: Medium_
AIID: [`#28`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/28/)

On May 6, 2010, the US stock market experienced a dramatic crash known as the Flash Crash, where the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell nearly 1,000 points in approximately 20 minutes before partially recovering. In 2015, authorities arrested Navinder Singh Sarao, a 36-year-old futures trader operating from his parents' home in London near Heathrow Airport. Sarao was charged with wire fraud, commodities fraud, and spoofing for allegedly manipulating E-mini S&P 500 futures contracts using automated trading software. Starting in 2009, Sarao had modified commercially available trading software to place large sell orders that he would cancel before execution, creating artificial downward pressure on prices. On the day of the Flash Crash, his fake sell orders represented 20-29% of the entire sell-side order book on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange. Authorities allege his spoofing activities created extreme order imbalances that contributed to the market crash. Sarao reportedly made $879,018 in profits on the day of the Flash Crash and approximately $40 million total between 2010-2014. The case was discovered through a whistleblower tip, and authorities noted that Sarao continued his allegedly manipulative trading practices for nearly five years after the Flash Crash, despite warnings from the CME.

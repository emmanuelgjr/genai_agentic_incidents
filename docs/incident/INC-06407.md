### INC-06407  [↗](/incident/INC-06407.html)

**Bots Allegedly Made up Roughly Half of Twitter Accounts in Discussions Surrounding COVID-19 Related Issues**  
_2020-01-01 · real-world · Severity: Medium_
AIID: [`#243`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/243/)

Carnegie Mellon University researchers analyzed over 200 million tweets discussing COVID-19 and related issues since January 2020 and discovered that approximately 50% of accounts appeared to be bots, with 62% of the 1,000 most influential retweeters being bots. This represented a significantly higher level of bot activity than the typical 10-20% seen during contentious events like elections or natural disasters. The researchers identified bots using AI systems that analyzed accounts based on tweet frequency, follower counts, and apparent location changes. Among tweets specifically about 'reopening America,' 66% came from accounts that were possibly humans using bot assistants to amplify their messages, while 34% came from actual bots. The researchers found over 100 types of inaccurate COVID-19 stories being spread, with tweets about ending stay-at-home orders being the most frequent. The bot detection was based on indicators like tweeting more frequently than humanly possible, appearing in different countries within hours, using identical hashtags, and copy-pasted messaging. The researchers could not determine whether the bot activity originated from foreign nation-state actors or domestic sources within the US, but noted that the global nature of the pandemic created opportunities for countries and interest groups to advance political agendas through this misinformation campaign.

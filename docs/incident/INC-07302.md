### INC-07302  [↗](/incident/INC-07302.html)

**Facebook’s Automated Removal of Content Featuring Nudity-Containing Artworks Denounced as Censorship**  
_2018-05-01 · real-world · Severity: High_
AIID: [`#284`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/284/)

Multiple cultural institutions experienced systematic censorship when Facebook's automated content moderation algorithms blocked their advertisements featuring classical nude artworks. The Montreal Museum of Fine Arts had its promotional ads for a Picasso exhibition rejected because the images showed abstract breasts in Picasso's 'Femmes à la toilette' and other works. Australian auction house Mossgreen was blocked from advertising Charles Blackman's 'Women Lovers' painting. The Flanders Tourist Board had multiple posts featuring Peter Paul Rubens paintings removed, including 'The Descent from the Cross' showing a bare-chested Jesus. Vienna's Natural History Museum had a post featuring the 30,000-year-old 'Venus of Willendorf' statue removed as pornographic. French teacher Frederic Durand-Baissas had his account suspended for posting Gustave Courbet's nude painting, leading to a legal case. In response to ongoing censorship, Vienna's tourism board created an OnlyFans account in 2021 to showcase 'explicit' artworks from four museums for $4.99 per month. The incidents span multiple years and affected approximately 22 million French Facebook users and numerous cultural institutions globally. Facebook acknowledged the issue and stated it was reviewing its nudity policies for artwork in advertisements.

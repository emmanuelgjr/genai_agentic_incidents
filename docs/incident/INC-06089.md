**Tesla Model S on ACC Crashed into Tree in Texas, Killing Two People**  
_2021-04-17 · real-world · Severity: Critical_
AIID: [`#337`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/337/)

On April 17, 2021, a 2019 Tesla Model S crashed into a tree and caught fire in Spring, Texas, killing two men aged 59 and 69. Harris County authorities initially reported that no one was in the driver's seat at the time of the crash, with one victim found in the front passenger seat and another in the back seat. The crash occurred at 11:25 p.m. on a curve in a residential area, with the vehicle traveling at high speed before hitting a tree about 600 feet from the driveway. The fire took four hours to extinguish, requiring 32,000 gallons of water due to the lithium battery repeatedly reigniting. Tesla CEO Elon Musk stated that data logs showed Autopilot was not enabled and the car had not purchased Full Self-Driving capability. However, Tesla later revealed that Adaptive Cruise Control was engaged but disengaged when the driver's seatbelt was unbuckled. Tesla also claimed the deformed steering wheel suggested someone was in the driver's seat at impact, contradicting initial police findings. Both NHTSA and NTSB launched investigations into the incident.

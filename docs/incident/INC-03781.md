### INC-03781  [↗](/incident/INC-03781.html)

**Faulty AI Transcription Threatens Integrity of Genoa Bribery Probe**  
_2024-05-26 · real-world · Severity: High_
AIID: [`#708`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/708/)

On May 26, 2024, in the office of preliminary investigation judge Paola Faggioni in Genoa, an audio recording of Roberto Spinelli's interrogation was re-examined after his lawyers requested clarification of a crucial detail in a corruption investigation involving Liguria region president Giovanni Toti. The transcript showed Spinelli had spoken of 'illegal financing' given to Toti, which would have confirmed prosecution allegations and was highlighted in media coverage. However, upon re-listening to the audio, it was discovered that Spinelli had actually said the exact opposite: 'legal financing.' The error originated from a speech-to-text software system used by the prosecutor's office to automatically transcribe interrogations. These AI transcription systems have been adopted by many Italian courts and prosecutor's offices in recent years due to budget cuts that reduced staff and transcribers. The systems are trained on thousands of hours of audio from TV programs, court hearings, and political debates, creating probabilistic models that can be influenced by cultural biases in the training data. Legal experts note that if 'financing' is frequently associated with illegal conduct in the training data, the system may predict 'illegal' as more probable than 'legal' when transcribing unclear audio.

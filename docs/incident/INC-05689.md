### INC-05689  [↗](/incident/INC-05689.html)

**Hawaii Police Deployed Robot Dog to Patrol a Homeless Encampment**  
_2021-01-10 · real-world · Severity: High_
AIID: [`#207`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/207/)

The Honolulu Police Department (HPD) purchased a Boston Dynamics Spot robot for $150,045 using federal CARES Act funding intended for pandemic relief. The robot was deployed at the Keehi Lagoon Beach Park homeless encampment to take temperatures of unhoused individuals as COVID-19 screening, replacing human interaction with robotic surveillance. According to deployment logs from September-November 2021, the robot was regularly used for 'temperature' and 'dinner' duties, though it occasionally lost signal or couldn't deploy due to weather conditions. HPD officials claimed the robot could save $117,000-$242,760 over 90 days by reducing officer exposure risks and overtime costs. Training documents indicate the robot is prohibited from weaponization but may be used for 'other duties' after the pandemic ends, including potential tactical operations with commander approval. Civil rights advocates, including the ACLU of Hawaii, criticized the purchase as dehumanizing treatment of vulnerable populations and raised concerns about future surveillance uses. The purchase was part of broader CARES Act spending by HPD totaling approximately $40 million, which exceeded the city's $25 million Household Relief Fund for individuals and families in need.

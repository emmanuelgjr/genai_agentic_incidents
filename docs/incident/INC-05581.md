### INC-05581  [↗](/incident/INC-05581.html)

**DUI Arrest Case Allegedly Based Only on ShotSpotter's Alert**  
_2021-11-07 · real-world · Severity: High_
AIID: [`#256`](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/256/)

ShotSpotter operates a gunshot-detection technology system using acoustic sensors that identify gunshots and trigger police alerts. On November 7, 2021, a ShotSpotter alert was recorded near Hamlin and Lake Street in Chicago's Garfield Park area, prompting at least eight Chicago police officers to respond. Two officers arrested a defendant a few blocks away on Central Park Avenue for DUI. The Cook County Public Defender Office subpoenaed ShotSpotter as a third party to obtain documents about the alert that led to the arrest, including analyst qualifications, training materials, instances of alert reclassification, methods for reclassification, data on sensor misidentification of gunfire, and data on missed gunshots. Instead of complying with the subpoena, ShotSpotter's attorneys requested the judge hold the company in 'friendly' contempt of court to allow them to appeal the discovery order. The sensors can incorrectly identify fireworks and other noises as gunshots, and the company employs analysts who review alerts in real time and can reclassify them retroactively. A separate civil lawsuit filed by Lucy Parsons Labs cited cases where ShotSpotter alerts led to arrests, including a 65-year-old grandfather held in jail for nearly a year before his case was dismissed due to insufficient evidence.
